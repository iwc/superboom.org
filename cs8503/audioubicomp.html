The Use of Audio in Ubiquitous Computing

Irwin W. Coleman III

        Computing is moving away from the machine rooms and the desktop. For years, computing devices lurk in our automobiles, in microwave ovens, and even in wristwatches. More recently, significant computing power has been make available in notebook computers and pocket sized PDAs. In the near future, even greater computing power will be embedded in our everyday household items, in our offices, and even worn like clothing. Migration from  the traditional workstation computing, using character oriented or GUI interfaces is mandatory. A computer in the house or office may be used in such a manner that a screen cannot easily be made available, or it may be impractical to provide a standard graphical interface in the situations where this new computing power is used. Portable computing devices and worn computing devices by defnition lack the physical dimensions needed for traditional displays, and the standard keyboard and mouse are entirely impratical in a truly mobile computing system. How do system designers then provide the necessary interaction these new applications require?

        One solution is to use audio. Hearing is our second most used sense, behind vision, and speech is our most common mode of communication. Also, audio output does not require focus on a screen, and audio input does not require a user to sit at a keyboard. The strategic placement of speakers and microphones in a computing-enhanced environment allow users to interact with a system while their hands and eyes may be occupied elsewhere. A system with these modes of interaction can be used or ignored as the user desires. This sort of task migratability is a vital part of the ubiquitous computing experience.
