Summary: The Context Toolkit: Aiding the Development of Context-Enabled Applications

Authors: Daniel Salber, Anind K. Dey, Gregory D. Abowd

Overview

This paper discusses the design issues and mechanisms necessary to support rapid ubiquitous application development. The Context Toolkit described in this paper presents several software abstractions to provide a layer on top of various sensor equipment and expose higher level events to ubiquitous application designers. Each unit of the toolkit, referred to as "widgets," provides access to some form of context awareness.

One of the example "context widgets" include IdentityPresence, which takes GPS or tracker data, and tracks the arrival and leaving times of users and can be coordinated with several other IdentityPresence for detailed location data of people in a space. Another "context widget" is Activity, which uses ambient noise levels, video image processing, or computer activity levels to measure an overall activity level and changes in the activity level. Information from these widgets can be combined to determine higher levels of context awareness. For example, the IdentityPresence and the Activity widgets can be used to detect the occurrence of meetings.

The paper also addresses example applications developed with the Toolkit, and related work.

Interesting Ideas/Questions

  • Extracting low level sensor data, and exposing higher level information to applications seems to be a critical component of ubicomp.
  • (Not directly related to the paper...) One of the obvious information sources in audio, speech recognition, still has limited accuracy, especially in real-world (noisy) environments.
  • The above begs the question: What information can be extracted and exposed from auditory sensors and processing?
    • What can be done as far as Computational Perception?
    • If the above is difficult to do, what limitations can simplify the problem (fixed set of voices to recognize, applications that do not rely on perfect speech recognition)?
    • What meta-information can audio information provide that other sensory channels cannot?
    • How can other context widgets work with an audio context widget to solve some of the ambiguity problems of speech recognition.
