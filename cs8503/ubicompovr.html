Summary: Ubiquitous Computing: Defining an HCI Research Agenda for an Emerging Interaction Paradigm

Authors: D. Salber, A. K. Dey, G. D. Abowd

Overview

This paper provides a definition of Ubicomp, and notes the primary concepts that define various ubicomp applications.

Axes for the Evaluation of Systems

Systems may be classified in terms of two attributes in relation to ubiquitous computing (Weiser, 1991):
  • ubiquity, which is a measure of availability to the user (Is the system available at all times, or only in an office?)
  • transparency, which is a measure of user perception of the presence of an interface (Does the user have to consciously manipulate the system, or is the interface cleanly integrated into familiar tasks?)

    •  
From another point of view, systems can be be evaluated against the axes of:
  • User mobility- Is the system portable, or always accessible within a defined space?
  • Interaction Transparency- Does the user have to consciously translate desired actions into the sublanguage of an interface?

    • User Mobility

    Systems can be considered either fully mobile or provide constrained mobility. Contrained mobility typically involved applications that are available in a large, but limited space, such as an intelligent home or office. Fully mobile systems are in some way portable (either the entire system or some sort of portable networked interface to a larger, stationary system). This sort of system included PDAs and wearable computing. Augmented reality applications tend to straddle the two categories due to the current hardware requirements for many AR systems.

    Interface Transparency

    Systems may address syntactic transparency and/or semantic transparency. Syntactic transparency means that a system provides a very natural means of interaction for users to articulate their desires. The notepad metaphor of many PDAs is an example of a system with some degree of syntactic transparency. Output mechanisms such as non-obtrusive power lights on computers are also elements that provide syntactic transparency, since the user need only be aware of them when needed. Semantic transparency addresses the ability of a system to determine user intentions or desires, and act on them intelligently with no direct user interaction. Motion sensing lights are a system with semantic transparency.

Mechanisms to Provide Mobility and Transparency

Systems may provide the high levels of Mobility and Transparency desired through Context-Awareness and Capture, Integration and Access.

Context-Awareness

Systems must harness various mechanisms to provide context-awareness. Understanding of task organization can be used to provide speculative work by a system. Computational perception may be employed to discern a user's identity or emotional state for system behavior customization, and the use of networks of sensors (position trackers, identification tags) can be used to provide location-aware customizations.
 

Capture, Integration and Access

Systems may use audio recording, video recording, and still image recording to record events that occur around a user, or within a space. If a system provides a meaningful way to access that information, then suddenly the system can provide an advanced extension of human memory. Information that might otherwise pass unnoticed can now be used at some later time. That information may be useful as raw data, or as input for Context-awareness purposes.

Conclusions

This paper provides a wonderful overview of what ubicomp means, and what forms it may take. It also addresses that aspects of ubicomp systems that make them useful in ways that traditional desktop-bound systems have not.
