Summary: Designing Voice-only Applications in the Absence of Speech Recognition Technology

Authors: Anind K. Dey, Lara D. Catledge, Gregory D. Abowd and Colin Potts
 

Overview

This paper describes a user study of speech interaction techniques in a "Wizard of Oz" implementation of a information access system. The system provides access to messaging, weather reports and stock market results. Due to the limitations of speech recognition technology, a "Wizard of Oz" approach replaces the speech recognition technology in this implementation.
 

Interesting Results

A number of interesting observations regarding guidelines for audio-only interaction:
  • When users believed that a computer was performing the speech recognition, users tended to phrase requests as commands, and avoid "please" and "thank you" courtesies that were used when humans were known to be performing the recognition. Commands expressed to humans were phrased with more informal language than computer-directed requests.
  • As users experienced success with simple requests, their requests became increasingly more complex. In other words, users took full advantage of the interface's abilities as they became aware of them.
  • Auditory responses to user requests can quickly and easily overburden the user cognitively. The authors suggest the use of "scaffolding" to ease the burden for both input and output. "Scaffolding" implies that system prompting varies based on the complexity of the task in progress, and with the level of success the user is having with the task in progress.
  • Users could not tolerate delays of greater than about five seconds. The audio interaction model provides limited means for progress feedback (such as progress bars in WIMP interfaces), so it is important to minimize input to output latencies.
  • Higher levels of user satisfaction occurred when users were presented with short, precise answers and a method to access more detailed information, in contrast to providing fully detailed information immediately in response to a query. This user preference seems to be linked to the cognitive loading issues discussed earlier.
