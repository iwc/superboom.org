Summary: Design of Spatialized Audio in Nomadic Environments

Authors: Nitin Sawhney, Chris Schmandt
 

Goals

The basic goals of the Nomadic Radio project are to provide an environment for the presentation of auditory information to users in a mobile wearable computing environment, and to provide a system of interaction for the user to examine and select from the various audio streams. Message retrieval and news updates were the primary problem domain of Nomadic Radio.

The Spacialized Radio Metaphor

The Nomadic Radio designers based the interaction paradigm on interaction techniques used with traditional radios. First, audio streams are broadcast to the user from some fixed location, where a computation environment of sufficient power can be housed. Second, the user may browse audio streams by selecting a message category, and using a forward/back navigation model to cycle through the audio streams in that category. Finally, the user may scan audio streams by previewing short segments of each audio stream. Each segment is faded in as the previous segment is faded out, providing more rapid access to the entire category of audio streams.

The primary tool used to focus the user's attention is audio spacialization. Spacialization cues are used to encode the arrival time of the audio stream and to indicate a selection by the user. Audio streams are spacialized so that the perceived sound sources lie within a planar orbit around the user's head. Each audio streams' orbital position corresponds to the location on a clock face of the arrival time of that audio stream. In other words, an audio stream that arrives at twelve o'clock would be spacialized straight ahead of the user's head, while a stream that arrived at nine o'clock would be spacialized directly to the left of the user's head. In the scanning operation, each audio segment is shifted from its orbital position to a center position, indicating that segment as the main focus of attention. The primary problem with the clock-oriented positioning is the overlap of audio streams that arrive in the AM, and others that arrive at the same hour, but in the PM. The two possible solutions to this ambiguity are an AM/PM indicator on each stream, or the use of a 24-hour clock. Each solution has its drawbacks, however.

Implementation Environment

A PC prototype exists for Nomadic Radio, but a more complete implementation also exists, using a wearable speaker array developed by Nortel and a wearable Intel PC. The RSX 3D libaries are used to provide a Java software implementation of the spacialization engine. UNIX workstations act as the collectors of information for broadcast, and a speech recognition API from AT&T is used for user input.

Other Interesting Items

A concept discussed in this paper, but is not clearly involved in the Nomadic Radio project is the concept of combining spacialization with head tracking as a selection metaphor. An audio signal of interest can be easily focused upon by the user, simply be relying on the natural human inclination to turn towards a stimulus of interest. This concept is worthy of some further investigation.
