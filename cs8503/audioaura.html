Summary: Designing Audio Aura

Authors: Elizabeth D. Mynatt, Maribeth Back, Roy Want, Michael Baer, Jason B. Ellis
 

Overview

Audio Aura is a system intended to provide ambient audio cues to inhabitants of an intelligent office space. Those ambient audio cues inform the inhabitants of the location or recent location of fellow employees, announce the arrival of electronic mail, and provide a background work level that includes the awareness of meetings and the manipulation of common data.

User Tracking

Each of the employees wear an Active Badge which broadcasts a signal to an array of sensors. Detection of a particular Active Badge by a sensor causes a database record to be stored about the wearer of the particular badge. When other users enter the same location at a later time, that database data may be queried to provide an appropriate sound event to inform a user that a coworker "just left" that area of the office. Similar database data is kept about an individuals incoming email, and location data can be aggregated to determine the occurence of meetings or group activity, which will affect the "group pulse" sounds which describe the office-wide work level.

Determination and Presentation of Audio Events

Auditory events are determined using the database information collected by several "pollers," which are machines which collect data from a particular set of sensors. Audio events are then presented to the user using a wireless headphone system.

Ambient Information Channels

The primary goal of this project is to provide users with unobtrusive information about their environment. Users are made aware of changes in the work level, the movements of other employees, and the arrival of email in a form that allows him or her to "tune out" events when those events are not considered important. To provide this aspect, the designers avoided the "alarm paradigm," where sounds invade the listener's attention with a "sharp attacks, high volume levels, and substantial frequency content in the same general range as the human voice."

Semantic Sound Schemes

The designers also examined three basic semantic approaches to the sound events: Sound Effects(a "sonic ecology"), music (or "earcons"), and voice. Early user studies provided positive reactions to such sound cues, with the most information coming from a combination of the three semantic approaches.

Future Work

Later work may involve privacy issues for data, such as the location of employees, and improvements on the intrusiveness of the tracking and audio hardware.
